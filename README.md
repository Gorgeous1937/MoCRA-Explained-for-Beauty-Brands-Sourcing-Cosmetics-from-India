# MoCRA-Explained-for-Beauty-Brands-Sourcing-Cosmetics-from-India
What the Modernization of Cosmetics Regulation Act means for US beauty brands that manufacture in India, and what to ask your manufacturer.

If you sell cosmetics in the United States and manufacture them in India, you have probably heard the word MoCRA. This article explains in plain language what it is, who is responsible for what, and which questions to put to your manufacturer.

Editor note: this article is general information, not legal advice. Verify every regulatory statement against current FDA text before publishing, because deadlines and rules change.

## What is MoCRA?
MoCRA is the Modernization of Cosmetics Regulation Act of 2022. It updated the Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act and gave the FDA stronger oversight of cosmetics sold in the US. It added requirements around facility registration, product listing, safety substantiation, adverse event reporting and labeling.

## Does the FDA approve cosmetics?
No. The FDA does not approve cosmetic products before they go on sale. It can take action against products that are adulterated or misbranded. This is why a factory certificate, or a claim that a product is FDA approved, should never be treated as proof of compliance.

## Who is the Responsible Person?
The Responsible Person is the manufacturer, packer or distributor whose name appears on the product label. For most start-up brands this is the brand owner. The Responsible Person is accountable for product listing, safety substantiation and serious adverse event reporting, even if production happens at a factory in another country.

## The main requirements at a glance
• Facility registration. Facilities that manufacture or process cosmetics for distribution in the US need to be registered with the FDA, unless an exemption applies. Foreign facilities are generally expected to name a US agent.
• Product listing. Each product is listed with the FDA, including its ingredients.
• Safety substantiation. The Responsible Person must keep adequate evidence that the product is safe under its intended conditions of use.
• Adverse event reporting. Serious adverse events must be reported to the FDA within the required period, and records must be kept.
• Labeling. Labels must include a US contact for adverse event reports (such as an address, phone number or electronic contact), along with the existing requirements such as statement of identity, net quantity and ingredient list.
• Good manufacturing practice. MoCRA directs the FDA to set GMP regulations for cosmetics. Check the current status of that rule.

## Is there a small-business exemption?
MoCRA exempts qualifying small businesses from some requirements, such as facility registration and product listing. The exemption does not cover certain product types, for example products that regularly contact the mucous membrane of the eye. Check your sales figures and product types against the FDA text before assuming you qualify.

## What this means when you manufacture in India
Manufacturing abroad does not change your obligations as the brand owner. What it changes is the coordination. You need your manufacturer to provide the full ingredient list with INCI names, color additive information, formula details your safety assessor needs and records of testing. You also need clear agreements on who handles each task.

## Questions to ask your manufacturer
1.  Is the facility registered with the FDA, and can you share the registration details?
2.  Which quality system does the factory follow (for example ISO 22716), and can you see the certificate and scope?
3.  Can you receive the complete ingredient list with INCI names and supplier documentation?
4.  What stability, microbial and preservative efficacy testing is done for my product type?
5.  How are batch records, complaints and out-of-specification results handled?
6.  How will changes to the formula or packaging be communicated and approved?
## Common myths
• Myth: my product is FDA approved because the factory is certified. A certificate describes the factory's quality system. It is not an approval of your product.
• Myth: the manufacturer handles all compliance. The Responsible Person has legal duties that cannot be handed over by contract alone.
• Myth: a small brand has no obligations. Some small businesses are exempt from parts of the law, but not from everything, and some product types are excluded from the exemption.
